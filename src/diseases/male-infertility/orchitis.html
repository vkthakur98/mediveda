Orchitis

Orchitis is the inflammation of one or both testicles, often caused by infection. The condition can lead to pain, swelling, tenderness, and, in severe cases, fever or nausea. Orchitis can affect fertility if it results in damage to the testicular tissue, as the testes are responsible for sperm production and hormone regulation. In some cases, orchitis may also occur alongside epididymitis (inflammation of the epididymis), a condition called epididymo-orchitis.

Physiology of the Testicles

The testicles (or testes) are the male reproductive organs responsible for producing sperm and secreting testosterone. The testicles are housed in the scrotum and are critical for male fertility and sexual function. Orchitis interferes with the normal functioning of the testes by causing inflammation, which can result in reduced sperm production, hormonal imbalance, and in severe cases, infertility. The inflammation may disrupt the blood supply to the testicles and damage the seminiferous tubules, where sperm are produced.

Causes of Orchitis

Orchitis is typically caused by infections, but it can also result from trauma or other medical conditions:
Viral Infections: Mumps virus is one of the most common viral causes of orchitis, especially in post-pubertal males. Mumps orchitis typically affects both testicles and can lead to complications like infertility if severe. Other viruses like influenza, enterovirus, and HIV may also cause orchitis.

Sexually transmitted infections (STIs): Bacterial infections such as Chlamydia trachomatis and Neisseria gonorrhoeae can cause orchitis, particularly in sexually active men.

Urinary tract infections (UTIs): Infections caused by Escherichia coli and other bacteria that travel from the urinary tract to the testicles can lead to orchitis.

Trauma or Injury: Physical injury or surgery involving the testicles or groin area may lead to orchitis due to localized inflammation.

Post-surgical Complications: Orchitis can develop after surgeries such as vasectomy, hernia repair, or prostate surgery, where infection or inflammation in the reproductive tract occurs.

Chronic condition: Chronic conditions like tuberculosis, prostatitis, or other infections may cause orchitis as a secondary complication.

Other Causes: Rarely, autoimmune diseases, testicular cancer, or a compromised immune system can lead to orchitis.
