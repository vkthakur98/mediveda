Oligospermia

Oligospermia is a condition characterized by a low sperm count in a man's semen, which can lead to male infertility. A normal sperm count is typically considered to be above 15 million sperm per milliliter of semen, and anything below this threshold is categorized as oligospermia. The condition can vary in severity, from mild oligospermia (slightly lower sperm count) to severe oligospermia (significantly low sperm count or even absent sperm).

Physiology of Oligospermia

Sperm are produced in the testes through a process known as spermatogenesis. These sperm are then stored in the epididymis until they are ejaculated. The number of sperm in semen can be affected by a variety of factors, including testicular health, hormonal balance, and the functioning of the reproductive tract. In oligospermia, the testes do not produce enough sperm to achieve natural fertilization, which can lead to difficulties in conception.

Causes of Oligospermia

Oligospermia can result from a wide range of causes including:
Hormonal Imbalances: Low levels of key reproductive hormones, such as testosterone, luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), can impair sperm production.

Varicocele: Enlarged veins in the scrotum can elevate testicular temperature, damaging sperm production.

Infections: Infections in the reproductive system, such as epididymitis or orchitis, can affect sperm quality and quantity.

Infections or scarring in the epididymis or vas deferens

Genetic Factors: Chromosomal disorders like Klinefelter syndrome, or microdeletions on the Y chromosome, can lead to impaired sperm production.

Lifestyle Factors: Exposure to toxins, drugs (e.g., recreational drugs, steroids), smoking, excessive alcohol consumption, and a sedentary lifestyle can all decrease sperm count

Environmental Factors: Heat exposure (e.g., frequent use of hot tubs, saunas), environmental pollutants, or radiation can disrupt sperm production.

Age: Sperm count and quality typically decline with age, particularly after the age of 40.

Obstructions:Blockages in the male reproductive tract can limit the flow of sperm, even if sperm production is normal.
