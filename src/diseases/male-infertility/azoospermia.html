Azoospermia

Azoospermia is the condition where no sperm is present in a man's semen, leading to male infertility. It can be classified into two main types: obstructive azoospermia, where sperm production is normal but blocked in the reproductive tract, and non-obstructive azoospermia, where sperm production is impaired or absent due to issues in the testes.

Physiology of Azoospermia

Sperm are produced in the testes through a process called spermatogenesis and are stored in the epididymis. During ejaculation, sperm travel through the vas deferens and mix with seminal fluid. In azoospermia, either sperm production is deficient, or the sperm cannot reach the semen due to blockages or other abnormalities.

Causes of Azoospermia

Obstructive Azoospermia: This occurs when sperm cannot pass through the reproductive tract due to blockages. Common causes include:

Congenital absence of the vas deferens (often linked to cystic fibrosis)

Vasectomy (surgical sterilization)

Infections or scarring in the epididymis or vas deferens

Non-Obstructive Azoospermia: This type is caused by defects in sperm production within the testes. Causes include:

Genetic conditions (e.g., Klinefelter syndrome, Y chromosome microdeletions)

Hormonal imbalances (e.g., low testosterone or FSH) Testicular failure due to infection (e.g., mumps), varicocele, trauma, or environmental factors like toxins and radiation.
