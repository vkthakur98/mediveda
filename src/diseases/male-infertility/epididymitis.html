Epididymitis

Epididymitis is an inflammation of the epididymis, a coiled tube located at the back of the testicle that stores and carries sperm. This condition can cause pain, swelling, and tenderness in the scrotum, and it may lead to fertility issues if left untreated. Epididymitis can be acute (sudden onset) or chronic (long-lasting).

Physiology of Epididymitis

The epididymis plays a key role in sperm maturation and transport. Sperm produced in the testes move to the epididymis, where they mature and are stored until ejaculation. In epididymitis, the inflammation disrupts this process, leading to pain, swelling, and sometimes blockage in the epididymis, which can affect sperm transport. If severe or untreated, epididymitis can lead to scarring or damage to the epididymis, potentially resulting in infertility.

Causes of Epididymitis

The main causes of epididymitis are infections, though other factors can contribute to the condition. Common causes include:
Bacterial Infections: The most frequent cause, often stemming from urinary tract infections (UTIs) or sexually transmitted infections (STIs), such as Chlamydia or Gonorrhea. The bacteria ascend from the urethra to the epididymis.

Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs): Infections that affect the bladder or prostate can spread to the epididymis, causing inflammation.

Prostatitis: Inflammation of the prostate gland, often due to bacterial infection, can also lead to epididymitis.

Viral Infections: Mumps virus is a known cause of viral epididymitis, especially in young boys who have not been vaccinated.

Trauma or Injury: Physical injury to the scrotum or groin can lead to inflammation of the epididymis.

Chemical Irritation: Use of urinary catheters or certain medications may irritate the epididymis and lead to inflammation.

Non-infectious causes: In some cases, epididymitis can occur without infection due to conditions like autoimmune disorders or vasculitis (inflammation of blood vessels).

Symptoms of Epididymitis

  • Pain and swelling in one or both testicles
  • Redness or warmth in the scrotal area
  • Pain during urination or ejaculation
  • Fever and chills (in acute cases)
  • Tenderness in the groin or lower abdomen
