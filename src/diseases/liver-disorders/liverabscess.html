Liver Abscess


Liver Abscess: A Brief Overview

A liver abscess is a localized collection of pus in the liver, typically caused by an infection. The condition leads to the formation of a cavity filled with pus, which can be the result of bacterial, parasitic, or fungal infections. It can present as a single abscess (solitary) or multiple abscesses (multilocular). If untreated, a liver abscess can lead to serious complications, including liver failure, sepsis, or peritonitis (infection in the abdominal cavity).

Physiology of Liver Abscess

The liver is an essential organ responsible for detoxification, protein synthesis, and bile production. It is well-supplied with blood, which makes it susceptible to infections. In the case of a liver abscess, infection can lead to inflammation of the liver tissue. The body responds by sending immune cells to the site of infection, leading to the accumulation of pus (a mixture of dead tissue, bacteria, and white blood cells). Over time, the infection can form a well-defined, encapsulated space within the liver, creating an abscess.

Causes of Liver Abscess

  • Bacterial Infections: The most common cause, with pathogens such as Escherichia coli (E. coli), Klebsiella pneumoniae, and Enterococcus.
  • Parasitic Infections: Amebic liver abscesses caused by the parasite Entamoeba histolytica.
  • Fungal Infections: Candida and Aspergillus species, particularly in immunocompromised individuals.
  • Biliary Obstruction: Gallstones or biliary strictures leading to infection in the liver.
  • Post-Surgical or Post-Trauma Infections: Infection spreading to the liver after surgery or trauma.
  • Systemic Infections: Septicemia causing liver abscesses.
  • Chronic Medical Conditions: Diabetes and cirrhosis increasing the risk of liver abscesses.

Symptoms of Liver Abscess

  • Fever and chills
  • Abdominal pain, particularly in the upper right quadrant
  • Jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes)
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Fatigue and loss of appetite
  • Enlarged liver (hepatomegaly)
  • Weight loss, dark urine, and pale stools

Diagnosis of Liver Abscess

  • Clinical Examination: Palpating the abdomen to check for signs of liver enlargement or tenderness.
  • Blood Tests: Elevated white blood cell count, liver function tests, and infection markers.
  • Imaging Tests: Ultrasound, CT scan, or MRI to detect abscesses in the liver.
  • Aspiration and Culture: Aspiration of pus for bacterial or parasitic culture.
