Cervical Spondylosis: An Overview

Cervical spondylosis, commonly referred to as cervical osteoarthritis or neck arthritis, is a degenerative condition that affects the cervical spine (the neck region of the spine). It is a condition marked by the breakdown of cartilage and bones in the neck joints, leading to pain, stiffness, and a reduced range of motion. Cervical spondylosis can result in a variety of symptoms such as neck pain, headaches, numbness, tingling in the arms, and in severe cases, nerve compression that can lead to weakness or loss of sensation in the arms or hands.

Physiology of Cervical Spondylosis

The cervical spine is made up of seven vertebrae (C1 to C7) and the intervertebral discs that sit between them, acting as cushions or shock absorbers. These discs allow the spine to move flexibly and absorb the impact during daily activities.

Cervical spondylosis occurs as part of the natural aging process, but it can also be influenced by other factors such as lifestyle, injury, or overuse. The main physiological changes in cervical spondylosis include:

  • Degeneration of Discs: The intervertebral discs lose water content and elasticity over time, causing them to shrink and become less effective as shock absorbers. This results in increased pressure on the vertebrae, leading to disc herniation or bulging in some cases.
  • Bone Spurs (Osteophytes): The body reacts to the degeneration of cartilage by forming bone spurs around the affected areas. These spurs can impinge on nerves and the spinal cord, leading to pain, numbness, or weakness in the arms and hands.
  • Stiffening of Ligaments: The ligaments in the cervical spine, which help maintain the structure of the neck, become less flexible and more prone to damage over time, leading to reduced movement and discomfort.
  • Nerve Compression: In severe cases, the degenerated discs or bone spurs can press on nearby nerves, causing tingling, numbness, or weakness in the arms, shoulders, or hands. This condition is known as cervical radiculopathy.
  • Reduced Blood Flow: As the structures in the cervical spine deteriorate, blood flow to the muscles and tissues of the neck may be reduced, leading to muscle weakness, stiffness, and pain.

Causes of Cervical Spondylosis

Several factors contribute to the development of cervical spondylosis. Some of the main causes include:

  • Aging: The discs in the spine lose hydration and elasticity over time, leading to degeneration.
  • Repetitive Strain and Poor Posture: Constant poor posture, especially while using computers or smartphones, can accelerate degeneration of the cervical spine.
  • Injury or Trauma: Injuries like whiplash can damage the discs, joints, or ligaments in the neck, leading to early degeneration.
  • Genetics: A family history of cervical spondylosis increases the risk of developing the condition.
  • Obesity: Excess body weight puts added pressure on the cervical spine, contributing to degeneration.
  • Sedentary Lifestyle: Lack of physical activity and poor ergonomics can promote degeneration.
  • Osteoarthritis: Osteoarthritis in the neck region is a common cause of cervical spondylosis.
